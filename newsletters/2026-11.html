Law & policy watch

  • The 2027 legislative session is coming. Bill filing opens November 9 and the session starts January 12, 2027. TEFA's current funding runs through August 2027, so lawmakers will decide its future budget this session.
  • TEFA under review. At a recent Senate Education Committee hearing, a senator questioned how the program's "homeschool/other" category is used. The Comptroller's office said every dollar must go to an approved educational expense. No change has been proposed yet; we'll share anything that affects homeschool families.
  • 2027–28 TEFA applications: no opening date has been announced. Join the state's interest list at educationfreedom.texas.gov to hear first.
  • Homeschool law itself hasn't changed. Texas homeschools are private schools; you still don't register, test or need a certificate. The basics in plain terms.

Tip of the month: keep a simple record

Texas doesn't require homeschool records, but a light one pays off later, for transcripts, a move to another school, or just seeing how far your kids have come.

  1. Once a week, jot down what each child worked on. Five minutes, a notebook is fine.
  2. Save two or three work samples per subject each semester. A photo on your phone counts.
  3. Keep a running list of books read and field trips taken.
  4. For high schoolers, start a transcript now: course, credits and grade, updated each semester.

Tackling a challenge: the November slump

The excitement of fall has worn off and the holidays are pulling at everyone. If school feels like a grind right now, you're in good company. A few things that help:

  • Shrink the day, not the year. Keep the core subjects and drop the extras for a couple of weeks.
  • Change the scenery. Take reading outside, to the library or to a park day.
  • Let a project carry the learning. One hands-on project can cover reading, math and writing at once.
  • Talk to other parents. A co-op or support group is the fastest cure for feeling alone. Post what you're looking for and let families near you find you.

Seasonal corner: Thanksgiving learning

  • Cook the math. Double a recipe, measure, convert and set the timer. Fractions have never tasted better.
  • A gratitude journal. One line a day through the month doubles as handwriting and writing practice.
  • Ask the elders. Have kids interview a grandparent about holidays growing up, then write or record the story.
  • Plan a lighter December. Many families school through mid-December and pick back up after New Year's. Decide now so the break feels earned, not guilty.
