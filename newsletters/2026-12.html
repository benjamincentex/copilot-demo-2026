Law & policy watch

  • TEFA bills are piling up. By November 17, more than a dozen bills to end or reshape Texas Education Freedom Accounts had been filed for 2027, including repeal bills and changes to eligibility and online learning. TEFA currently covers $2,000 in homeschool expenses, and nothing changes unless a bill passes. Filing runs through March 12; the session starts January 12. Longview News-Journal
  • More TEFA spending data is public. On November 20 the Comptroller added tuition spending, marketplace activity and spending by category to the program's transparency dashboard, with participant data masked. Expect lawmakers on all sides to cite these numbers this session. Texas Comptroller
  • 2027–28 TEFA applications: still no opening date announced. Watch educationfreedom.texas.gov for it.
  • Homeschool law and UIL access haven't changed this month. Texas homeschools are still private schools with no registration or testing. The basics in plain terms.

Tip of the month: teach several ages at one table

Running a separate lesson for every child wears out the teacher fast. Many subjects can be shared, with each kid working at their own level.

  1. Pick subjects that travel well across ages: history, science, read-alouds, art and music.
  2. Teach one topic to everyone, then split the follow-up. A 6-year-old draws and narrates, a 10-year-old writes a paragraph, a teen reads a primary source.
  3. Keep math and reading separate, since those build step by step.
  4. Give older kids a role. Reading to a younger sibling or running the experiment counts as learning too.
  5. Set up a quiet "while you wait" box (puzzles, audiobooks, a sketchbook) for whoever isn't with you.

Tackling a challenge: questions at the holiday table

Gatherings can bring the questions: what about friends, college, or "real" school? Most relatives ask because they care. A few ways to keep it friendly:

  • Have a one-sentence answer ready. Something like "It's working well for us, and we reassess every year" ends most conversations kindly.
  • Show, don't argue. Let your kids share a project, a favorite book, or what they saw on a field trip.
  • Name the people in their week. Co-op friends, a library club, a team. Socialization worries fade when there are names attached.
  • Find your own backup. Other homeschool families have heard every question. A support group near you is a good place to swap answers that worked.

Seasonal corner: a December that still teaches

  • Winter traditions around the world. Pick a few celebrations from different countries and learn one food, song or story from each.
  • Budget the gifts. Give older kids a set amount to plan, compare prices and track. That's real-world math.
  • Look up. December brings the longest nights of the year. Track sunset times through the month, or catch a local star party.
  • Do a five-minute mid-year check-in. Before the break, jot down what's working, what isn't, and one thing to change in January. Spring classes and co-op spots fill early, so it helps to know what you want.
