Texas Homeschool Starter Checklist

Everything you need to start homeschooling in Texas, in the order most families do it. Check things off as you go.

1. Know the basics of Texas law

  • Texas treats a homeschool as a private school (Texas Supreme Court, Leeper v. Arlington ISD, 1994).
  • Run it in a real, genuine way, using a written or visual curriculum: books, workbooks or online courses.
  • Cover reading, spelling, grammar, math and good citizenship.
  • There's no state registration, required testing, teaching certificate or set number of days.

2. Withdraw in writing (if your child is enrolled)

  • Send the school a short, signed and dated letter. You don't have to go in person or show your curriculum.
  • Keep a copy, and ask the school to confirm the withdrawal in writing.
  • If the district asks, you can give a one-time letter saying your curriculum covers the basic subjects.
Sample letter (adapt to your family): [Date] To [School name / Principal], Please withdraw [child's name], grade [_], from [school] effective [date]. We are homeschooling [him/her/them] as a private school, using a curriculum that covers reading, spelling, grammar, math and good citizenship. Sincerely, [Parent name and signature]

3. Choose your curriculum and rhythm

  • Pick materials for the five core subjects. Many families add science, history, art and music.
  • Decide on a simple weekly rhythm: which days are lessons, field trips, co-op or activities.
  • Give the first few weeks extra grace. Most families adjust after a month or two.

4. Keep simple records

  • Once a week, jot down what each child worked on.
  • Save 2–3 work samples per subject each semester (photos count).
  • Keep a list of books read and field trips taken.
  • For high schoolers, start a transcript: courses, credits and grades.

5. Find your people

  • Look for a co-op, support group or class near you in the directory, or take the 5-question quiz.
  • Add sports, music, swim or art from homeschool-friendly activities.
  • Check the calendar for open houses, deadlines and homeschool days.
  • Tap "Notify me" on groups that are full, so you hear when spots open.

6. Check TEFA funding

  • Texas Education Freedom Accounts give homeschool students in the program $2,000 a year for approved expenses.
  • Only approved providers can be paid; check the state's provider finder before you buy.
  • Join the interest list at educationfreedom.texas.gov to hear when applications open.

This checklist is general information, not legal advice. See Texas homeschool law in plain terms and the Texas Education Agency's guidance for details. © CenTex Homeschool Hub
