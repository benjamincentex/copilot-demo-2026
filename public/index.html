How to start homeschooling in Texas

Texas is one of the simplest states to homeschool in. Most families are up and running in a week or two.

  1. Know the basics of the law

    Homeschools are private schools in Texas. You don't register with the state, take required tests or need a teaching certificate. See the law section below.

  2. Withdraw in writing, if your child is enrolled

    Send the school a short, signed and dated letter saying you're homeschooling and when it starts. You don't have to go in person or show your curriculum.

  3. Choose a curriculum

    Cover reading, spelling, grammar, math and good citizenship using real materials: books, workbooks or online courses. Many families add science, history and the arts.

  4. Find your people

    A co-op, support group or class makes a big difference, especially in the first year. Take the quiz or browse the directory.

  5. Keep simple records

    Not required by the state, but a list of what you covered, samples of work and a running transcript in the high school years help later.

  6. Check TEFA funding

    Homeschool students in the program get $2,000 a year for curriculum, classes and tutoring from approved providers. See the TEFA section below.

Texas homeschool law

Texas treats a homeschool as a private school, based on the Texas Supreme Court's 1994 decision in Leeper v. Arlington ISD.

What the law requires

  • The homeschool must be run in a bona fide (real, not sham) way.
  • You use a written or visual curriculum: books, workbooks, worksheets or online materials.
  • It covers the basics: reading, spelling, grammar, math and good citizenship.

What it doesn't require

  • No registering with the state or your school district.
  • No state testing, teaching certificate or set number of days or hours.
  • A district may ask once, in writing, for a letter saying your curriculum covers the basic subjects. It doesn't need renewing each year.

Newer laws worth knowing (2025)

  • UIL access (SB 401): homeschool students can join UIL activities, like sports and band, at their local district unless the district opts out.
  • College access (HB 3041): equal treatment for homeschool graduates in public college admissions, financial aid and dual credit.
  • Homeschool Freedom Act (HB 2674): state education agencies can't add new homeschool rules without the Legislature's approval.
  • PSAT and AP tests: districts must let homeschool students take the PSAT and AP exams they offer to enrolled students.

This is general information, not legal advice. For your specific situation, check the sources in Helpful links below.

TEFA: Texas Education Freedom Accounts

Texas launched its education savings account program for the 2026–27 school year. Here are the basics.

$2,000

per year for each homeschooled student, a fixed amount regardless of grade. Students with disabilities can qualify for more.

  • Funds can go to curriculum, tutoring, classes, therapies, technology and more.
  • You can only pay providers that are registered and approved by the program.
  • Homeschool parents cannot pay themselves "tuition" from the account.

Before you spend TEFA funds

  • Check that the tutor, class or program is approved using the state's provider finder.
  • Ask the provider whether they accept payment through the TEFA family portal.
  • Sign up for our monthly newsletter for a reminder when the next application window opens.

Rules and amounts can change. Always confirm on the official TEFA site before you spend.

Frequently asked questions

Is homeschooling legal in Texas?

Yes. Homeschools are legally private schools in Texas, and homeschooled children are exempt from public school attendance the same way private school students are.

Do I need to register or notify anyone?

No state registration exists. If your child is currently enrolled in a public school, send a written withdrawal letter so the school records them as homeschooled. If they've never been enrolled, there's nothing to file.

Do I need a teaching degree?

No. Texas has no teacher qualification requirement for homeschool parents.

Are there required tests?

No. Many families choose an annual standardized test to track progress, and homeschool students can take the PSAT and AP exams through their local district.

How many days or hours do we have to school?

Texas doesn't set a number for homeschools. Most families follow a school-year rhythm, but you choose the schedule.

What about high school and diplomas?

As the private school, you issue your student's transcript and diploma. Keep a transcript with courses, grades and credits as you go. Dual credit through a community college is a popular option, and a 2025 law guarantees homeschoolers equal treatment in public college admissions.

Can my kids play sports or join band at the public school?

Often, yes. Under a 2025 law, homeschool students can join UIL activities at their local district unless that district opts out. Ask your district about its sign-up steps.

How do we make friends and find community?

That's what this site is for. Co-ops, support groups, park days and classes are everywhere in Central Texas. Try the quiz, the calendar or the community board.

How much does homeschooling cost?

Anywhere from nearly free, using the library and free online materials, to a few thousand dollars a year with paid classes or a hybrid school. TEFA funds can cover $2,000 a year of approved costs for students in the program.
